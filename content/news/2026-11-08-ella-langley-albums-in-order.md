If you came to Ella Langley through "Choosin' Texas" or the Riley Green duet and went looking for the rest, the catalog can be confusing fast. She released two studio albums in under two years, each one picked up a deluxe or reissue that added songs, and a pile of her biggest moments are features on other artists' records. So here is every Ella Langley album in order, from the 2023 EP that started it to the 2026 album that made her the biggest name in country, with release dates, the singles that came off each one, and a plain answer to where you should start.

## Ella Langley albums in order, at a glance

Here is the quick chronological version, oldest to newest:

- **Excuse the Mess** - EP, 2023
- **Hungover** - debut studio album, August 2, 2024
- **Still Hungover** - deluxe edition of Hungover, November 2024
- **Dandelion** - second studio album, April 10, 2026
- **Dandelion (reissue)** - expanded edition, April 24, 2026

That is two proper studio albums, one early EP, and two expanded editions that added tracks to records she had already put out. Everything else with her name on it, and there is a lot of it in 2026, is a guest feature on someone else's project. We get to those at the bottom.

## Excuse the Mess (EP, 2023): the starting point

Before the arenas, before the award sweeps, there was a short EP called Excuse the Mess. It is the first Ella Langley release most people can find on streaming, and it is where she first sang with Koe Wetzel, on a duet called "That's Why We Fight" (the two of them would reunite three years later on his song "Jaded"). The EP is rawer and more traditional than what came next, and it mostly matters now as the on-ramp: this is the sound she built the debut album on.

## Hungover (2024): the debut

**Hungover** arrived August 2, 2024, produced start to finish by Will Bundy, and it is the record that made the case for her. She co-wrote every track, and the writing is the point: honky-tonk piano, heartbreak dressed up as a party, and a voice that sounds lived-in rather than polished.

The singles tell the story of her breakout. "nicotine" came first, back in March 2024, as a promotional single that still over-streams most of the album. The career-changer was ["you look like you love me"](/songs/you-look-like-you-love-me), the Riley Green duet released June 21, 2024, which became her first Billboard Hot 100 entry and her first No. 1 on Country Airplay. Then came ["weren't for the wind"](/songs/werent-for-the-wind), the quieter solo single Nashville heard and decided she was the real thing. That last one actually debuted a little after the album and landed on **Still Hungover**, the November 2024 deluxe edition that padded the record out with a handful of extra tracks. If you buy Hungover today, the deluxe is the one you want, because it holds both of the songs that broke her.

## Dandelion (2026): the breakthrough album

**Dandelion**, released April 10, 2026, is the album that turned a rising star into the biggest artist in the genre. She co-produced it with Ben West and Miranda Lambert, and the sound opens up: still country at the center, but with rock edges on cuts like ["bottom of your boots"](/songs/bottom-of-your-boots) and softer, searching moments like ["be her"](/songs/be-her). It debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200.

The lead single is the one that rewrote the record books. ["Choosin' Texas"](/songs/choosin-texas), written with Miranda Lambert, Luke Dick and Joybeth Taylor and released back in October 2025, became her first Hot 100 No. 1 and has since logged the most weeks at No. 1 in the chart's entire history. (We keep a running tally of [every Billboard record she has broken](/guides/ella-langley-billboard-records), because there are a lot of them.) The follow-up singles kept coming: "be her," the steel-soaked ballad that peaked at No. 2 behind only her own "Choosin' Texas"; the title track ["Dandelion"](/songs/dandelion), her small-town resilience anthem; and "loving life again," the recovery song she wrote after stepping away from the road in 2025.

Two weeks after release, on April 24, 2026, a reissue added "I Can't Love You Anymore," her duet with Morgan Wallen, which promptly went to No. 1 on Country Airplay. The album also holds duets with Miranda Lambert ("butterfly season") deep in the tracklist, so Dandelion is as much a collaboration record as a solo one.

## The features that aren't on her albums

A fair chunk of Ella Langley's 2026 chart presence is songs that live on other artists' records, which is why they never show up when you sort her two albums. The big ones, in order:

- **"Don't Mind If I Do"** with Riley Green (2024) - his song, their second duet, a Country Airplay No. 1.
- **"Never Met Anyone Like You"** with HARDY (2025) - a standalone single with a nasty twist in the bridge.
- **"Hell at Night"** with BigXthaPlug (2025) - the country-rap breakup song that hit the Hot 100 top 30.
- **"Here for the Party"** with Gretchen Wilson (2026) - a re-recording of Wilson's 2004 anthem.
- **"Jaded"** with Koe Wetzel (2026) - a call-and-response duet on the deluxe edition of his album.

## Which Ella Langley album should you start with?

If you only have time for one, start with **Dandelion**. It is the more polished, more ambitious record, and it holds the songs that are everywhere right now. If you want to understand how she got here, go back to **Hungover** (the Still Hungover deluxe) and hear the writer before the production got big. Play them in that order and you get the whole arc in two sittings: the honky-tonk debut that proved she could write, and the breakout that proved she could do anything.
