If you only watch country music one night a year, November 18 is the night, and the storyline writes itself: Ella Langley vs. Lainey Wilson. One of them is hosting the 60th CMA Awards for the third year running. The other walked in leading every artist in the building with nine nominations. They are the two names everybody keeps setting against each other this fall, and for once the comparison is not lazy — they are nominated against each other in three real categories. Here is the honest, side-by-side breakdown of where these two overlap, where they split, and how the head-to-head actually shakes out.

## The one-line version

Lainey Wilson is the reigning champ — the bell-bottomed, Louisiana-raised veteran who turned a decade of grinding into back-to-back Entertainer of the Year trophies and a standing invitation to run the whole show. Ella Langley is the challenger having the single biggest year in the room: a No. 1 album, a No. 1 all-genre hit, and a nomination haul that outnumbers everyone else on the ballot. Wilson owns the throne. Langley is the one most likely to take it.

## Where they came from

Lainey Wilson is from Baskin, Louisiana — a farming town of roughly 170 people — born in 1992. She spent years in a camper trailer in Nashville writing songs before "Things a Man Oughta Know" finally broke her through, climbing to No. 1 on Billboard's Country Airplay chart in 2021. Her 2022 album *Bell Bottom Country* won the Grammy for Best Country Album, and that same year she stepped onto *Yellowstone* as Abby, a musician character that put her face in front of millions who had never heard the records.

Ella Langley is from Hope Hull, Alabama, a speck of a place just south of Montgomery. Her breakthrough came through "You Look Lkke You Love Me," the Riley Green duet off *Hungover* that turned into one of the most-played country songs in the country. If you want the wider field both of them came up in, we mapped the whole generation in our [Ella Langley vs. the class of 2026 field guide](/guides/ella-langley-vs-the-class-of-2026).

So both are self-made Southern women who broke through on a single undeniable record. The gap between them is mostly time: Wilson is a few years and several trophies further down the road.

## The résumés, side by side

This is where the "they're the same" talk falls apart. Wilson is a four-time CMA Female Vocalist of the Year (2022 through 2025) and a two-time Entertainer of the Year, taking the genre's top prize in 2023 and again in 2025. She is, by any measure, the most decorated woman in mainstream country right now.

Langley's hardware is newer but arrived in an avalanche. In 2026 she swept all seven of her ACM Award nominations in a single night — a record — and then led the 2026 CMA field with nine nods, including her first-ever Entertainer of the Year nomination. Her sophomore album *Dandelion* debuted at No. 1, and its single ["Choosin' Texas"](/songs/choosin-texas) went all the way to the top of the all-genre Billboard Hot 100 and stayed there for weeks, a feat almost no country song by a woman has ever managed. ## The 2026 CMA head-to-head

Here is the part the broadcast will not spell out for you. Langley and Wilson are not competing in all nine of Ella's categories — they collide in exactly three.

**Entertainer of the Year.** The marquee race, and the one that matters most. The field is Luke Combs, Cody Johnson, Ella Langley, Chris Stapleton, and Lainey Wilson. Wilson is the two-time reigning winner going for a third; Langley is the first-time nominee riding the loudest year. If there is an upset coming, it is here.

**Female Vocalist of the Year.** Loaded: Miranda Lambert, Ella Langley, Megan Moroney, Kacey Musgraves, and Lainey Wilson. Wilson has won this four years straight. Langley is trying to end the streak in her first appearance on the ballot.

**Single of the Year.** Langley is double-nominated here — both ["Be Her"](/songs/be-her) and "Choosin' Texas" made the cut — against Wilson's "Somewhere Over Laredo," plus Taylor Swift and Cody Johnson. Splitting her own vote across two singles is the one place Langley's dominance could work against her.

Everywhere else, Ella is running alone. Wilson is not nominated in Album, Song, Music Video, or Musical Event of the Year — categories where Langley picked up five more nods, including Musical Event for ["I Can't Love You Anymore" with Morgan Wallen](/songs/i-cant-love-you-anymore). ## The sound

Résumés aside, they do not sing the same. Wilson's lane is swampy, Southern-rock-tinged country — "Bell Bottom Country" as a whole aesthetic, big grooves, a little grit, a lot of attitude. Langley pulls harder toward honky-tonk and traditional twang, and she loves a co-ed duet, trading verses with Riley Green, Koe Wetzel, and Morgan Wallen where Wilson usually commands the mic alone. If you want to hear the range before you pick a side, our [best Ella Langley songs, ranked](/guides/best-ella-langley-songs-ranked) is the fastest way in, and the [Ella Langley vs. Megan Moroney](/guides/ella-langley-vs-megan-moroney) head-to-head covers her other great rivalry of the moment.

## So who wins on November 18?

If you are asking who the safer bet is, it is still Lainey Wilson — champions do not usually get dethroned on vibes, and she has the voting body's trust. But if you are asking who the night belongs to on paper, it is Ella Langley: more nominations than anyone, the biggest commercial year of the bunch, and a genre quietly deciding whether it is ready to hand her the crown. The smart money says Wilson holds Entertainer of the Year one more time and Langley finally breaks through for a Female Vocalist or Single win. Watch the host hand someone a trophy she might have wanted for herself — that is the moment worth staying up for.
