Here's one we did not have on the summer bingo card: Ella Langley is a Dove Awards nominee. When the Gospel Music Association announced the nominees for the 57th Annual GMA Dove Awards this week, "Speaking Terms" showed up in the Country/Roots Song of the Year category — which means the quietest, most personal song on *Dandelion* just earned Ella recognition from the gospel music world, a lane she has never competed in before.

MusicRow and Billboard both carried the full nominee list, and Parade picked up the Ella angle Thursday morning. The ceremony happens October 6 at Bridgestone Arena in Nashville, with eight-time Dove winner Tauren Wells hosting for his fourth straight year.

## A country record in a gospel room

The Dove Awards are Christian and gospel music's biggest night, and the Country/Roots Song of the Year category is where the two worlds overlap. This year the overall field is led by Phil Wickham with nine nominations and CeCe Winans with seven — heavyweight gospel company for a woman whose biggest hit is about choosing a state over a man.

That's exactly what makes this nomination interesting to us. Ella didn't record a gospel record. She recorded a song about the distance between herself and God — the doubt, the silence, the feeling of not being on speaking terms — and the gospel world heard it as honest enough to nominate anyway. That says a lot about the song.

## Why "Speaking Terms" hit different

<Figure photo="white-embroidered-dress" caption="The Dandelion cut that reached the gospel world: 'Speaking Terms' peaked at No. 63 on the Hot 100 and No. 22 on Hot Country Songs." />

"Speaking Terms" has always been the odd one out on *Dandelion*, and Ella has said so herself. In a TikTok Live quoted by Parade, she explained it's the only outside cut she has ever released — a song written by someone else that was originally on hold for another artist — and that a moment during an Uber ride felt like a sign she was supposed to record it. "I never felt worthy of that song," she said. "I thought it was a special one. My relationship with my faith was very different from what it looks like now."

For a catalog built almost entirely on her own pen, letting an outside song through the door meant it had to matter. On the charts it did respectable, not record-breaking, numbers — a No. 63 debut on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 22 on Hot Country Songs, per Parade. But awards voters clearly heard past the peak position.

## The company she's keeping

The Country/Roots Song of the Year field is stacked with artists who live at the faith-and-country intersection full time: "Black Sheep" by Ben Fuller, "Broken Man Can" by Scotty Inman, "Still Do" by Anne Wilson with Cole Swindell, and "The Jesus I Know Now" by Brandon Lake with Lainey Wilson. Ella is the only nominee in the category coming off a mainstream country No. 1 — and given [the run "Choosin' Texas" has been on](/news/2026-07-28-ella-langley-choosin-texas-15-weeks-record) (full story on [the song page](/songs/choosin-texas)), she may be the name in the category most voters' kids know.
