If you've followed Ella Langley's rise and kept seeing one name turn up next to hers, you're not imagining it. Ask about the people who shaped this run and the Ella Langley and Miranda Lambert connection sits at the center of it — not as a one-off duet or a photo-op, but as a working partnership and a real mentorship that runs through the songs, the album credits, and some of the harder days behind the scenes.

Here's what Lambert has actually done for her, in her own words and on the record.

## What Miranda Lambert has taught Ella Langley

The advice fans keep coming back to is the bluntest one. Langley has said that during a rough stretch, Lambert told her plainly, "You are more important than this job." Langley described what that did for her to Parade: "She made me care for myself. Having someone like that made me feel like I'm okay." It's a small line, but it reframes the whole relationship — the mentorship people notice on stage started as someone older in the room telling a rising star to protect herself first.

She's said the same thing a broader way to Variety: "Miranda has given me more advice than I can possibly ask for. She's always reminded me to stay true to myself, keep the music at the center of everything, and trust that the rest will take care of itself." Keep the music at the center is a through-line in everything Langley says about her, and it shows up in how the two actually work together.

## It's not just advice — it's on the credits

The mentorship isn't abstract. Lambert is stitched into the biggest record of Langley's career. She's a co-writer on ["Choosin' Texas"](/songs/choosin-texas) alongside Langley, Luke Dick, and Joybeth Taylor, and she co-produced the song with Langley and Ben West. On the album that song leads, *Dandelion*, Lambert is credited as a co-producer and executive producer with Langley and West, and the two of them sing together on the track "Butterfly Season."

She turns up in front of the camera too — Lambert appears in the star-packed "Choosin' Texas" music video that Langley released earlier this year. So the friendship you see when they share a stage is backed by the least sentimental proof there is: her name in the liner notes. If you want the fuller story of what put Langley on the map, our breakdown of [what "Choosin' Texas" is actually about](/news/2026-07-22-choosin-texas-meaning) gets into the song the two of them built together.

## Why this mentorship matters right now

Langley has been open that she wants to pass the same thing forward. On her current headlining run she's put a rotating cast of newer artists in front of arena crowds, and when Variety asked about her own dream collaborators she named Stevie Nicks, Chris Stapleton, and Harry Styles — an answer that says a lot about how wide she wants her music to reach. The Lambert playbook, in other words, is one she's already running herself: help the next person up, and keep the songs at the center.

## The short version

Miranda Lambert didn't just cameo in Ella Langley's story. She co-wrote and co-produced the song that broke her wide open, helped steer the album around it, sings on it, and — by Langley's own account — talked her into taking care of herself when the job got heavy. That's the rare kind of mentorship that shows up in the music and in the person making it.
