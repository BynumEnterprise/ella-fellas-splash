If you follow country radio at all in 2026, "Ella Langley vs. Lainey Wilson" is a matchup you have probably typed into a search bar. It is easy to see why we get the question so often: two Southern women, both writing their own songs, both winning trophies, both showing up on every festival poster. But once you get past the surface, Ella Langley and Lainey Wilson are running very different races — different backgrounds, different sounds, and career timelines that are almost a decade apart. Here is how we read the two of them.

## Ella Langley vs. Lainey Wilson at a glance

| | Ella Langley | Lainey Wilson |
|---|---|---|
| From | Hope Hull, Alabama | Baskin, Louisiana |
| Breakthrough | "You Look Like You Love Me" (with Riley Green), 2024 | "Things a Man Oughta Know," 2021 |
| Signature album | *Dandelion* (2026) | *Bell Bottom Country* (2022) |
| Biggest single | "Choosin' Texas" — No. 1 Billboard Hot 100 | "Heart Like a Truck" — Country Airplay No. 1 |
| Also known for | Miranda Lambert mentorship, the Dandelion Tour | CMA Entertainer of the Year, *Yellowstone* role |

## Where they came from

Lainey Wilson did it the long way. She grew up on a farm in Baskin, Louisiana, moved to Nashville around 2011, and famously lived in a camper trailer for years while she chipped away at songwriting. Her first real radio moment, "Things a Man Oughta Know," didn't hit No. 1 on Country Airplay until 2021 — roughly a decade after she showed up in town. That grind is baked into how fans see her: she is the artist who paid dues in public.

Ella Langley's climb has been steeper and faster. She is from Hope Hull, Alabama, and broke through in 2024 with "You Look Like You Love Me," her duet with Riley Green, off her debut album *Hungover*. Two years later she released *Dandelion* and "Choosin' Texas," which we broke down in [the full story behind the song](/news/2026-07-22-choosin-texas-meaning). The gap matters: Lainey is a road-tested veteran of the format, and Ella is the newer arrival who reached the very top in record time.

## How they sound

This is where the "same lane" idea falls apart. Lainey Wilson leans into a swampy, Southern-rock-tinted country — bell-bottom bravado, a little grit, plenty of attitude. Her records feel built for a big stage and a bigger chorus.

Ella Langley sits closer to the traditional country heartbreak school. "Choosin' Texas" was written at a retreat with Miranda Lambert, Luke Dick, and Joybeth Taylor, and it plays like an old-school two-step lament dressed in modern radio polish. Lambert's fingerprints — she co-wrote and co-produced it, and sings background vocals — point to where Ella's instincts live: story-first, twang intact. If Lainey is the arena-rock end of the new class, Ella is the honky-tonk end.

## The numbers

On raw chart peaks, Ella has the louder 2026. "Choosin' Texas" became her first Billboard Hot 100 No. 1 and went on one of the longest runs at the top in the chart's history, crossing over well past country radio and topping charts in Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, and the U.K. It is a genuinely rare crossover for a country song by a woman.

Lainey's résumé is deeper rather than higher. She has stacked Country Airplay No. 1s — "Heart Like a Truck," "Watermelon Moonshine," her Cole Swindell duet "Never Say Never" — plus a CMA Entertainer of the Year win and a recurring role on *Yellowstone* that pulled in fans who don't follow the charts at all. Ella is currently out front on the singles chart; Lainey has the fuller trophy case built over more years. Both, notably, are in the awards conversation again heading into the 2026 CMAs, where Ella [leads the field of nominees](/news/2026-09-11-ella-langley-leads-2026-cma-nominations).

## Who should you see live

Different nights out. A Lainey Wilson show is a rowdy, rock-forward party — the kind of set built to fill an amphitheater lawn. Ella Langley's Dandelion Tour leans on the emotional swing of her catalog, the sing-along heartbreak, and the Lambert connection that shaped the record. If you want a barn-burner, Lainey. If you want to cry in the good way and then two-step it off, Ella. ## The bottom line

Ella Langley vs. Lainey Wilson isn't really a rivalry, and it isn't the same artist twice. Lainey is the decade-in-the-making veteran with the rock edge and the broadest résumé; Ella is the fast-rising traditionalist who just put up one of the biggest singles any country woman has ever charted. They pull from the same country roots and land in very different places — which is exactly why there's room for both of them at the top right now.
