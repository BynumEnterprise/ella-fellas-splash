If you spent any of 2026 with the country charts on, you ran into both of them, which is exactly why "Ella Langley vs. Megan Moroney" is one of the questions we get most. They came up in the same window, they both write like they mean it, and they have spent the year trading headlines. But they are not the same artist wearing different hats — the differences in where they come from, how they sound, and how the year has actually gone for each of them are bigger than the surface pairing suggests. Here is how we read the two of them.

## Side by side at a glance

| | Ella Langley | Megan Moroney |
|---|---|---|
| From | Hope Hull, Alabama | Savannah, Georgia |
| Breakthrough | "You Look Like You Love Me" (with Riley Green) | "Tennessee Orange" |
| Signature record (2026) | "Choosin' Texas" | *Cloud 9* (album) |
| Self-described lane | Traditional-leaning, Texas-red-dirt edge | "Emo cowgirl" |
| 2026 awards story | Leads the CMA nominations | Snubbed at the CMAs after leading the ACMs |

## Where they come from

Langley is an Alabama kid from tiny Hope Hull who built her name the long way, through Texas and red-dirt rooms before country radio caught up. Moroney is from Savannah, Georgia, and broke through faster: "Tennessee Orange" went triple platinum and, by her own account, brought a stack of record-label offers to her door almost overnight. That head start matters to the comparison. Moroney has a two-album head start on the mainstream — *Lucky* in 2023 and *Am I Okay?* in 2024, the latter debuting at No. 9 on the Billboard 200 — while Langley's rise has been steeper and more recent, cresting with the record-setting run of "Choosin' Texas." If you want the fuller version of Langley's origin, we lay it out in our [breakdown of the "Choosin' Texas" meaning](/news/2026-07-22-choosin-texas-meaning).

## Sound and style

This is where the "same artist" idea falls apart. Moroney calls herself an emo cowgirl and means it — her songs lean confessional and diaristic, pop-country in production, more likely to sit you down and read you a text-message postmortem of a breakup. Her stated influences run to writers like John Prine and Emmylou Harris, and you can hear the songwriter's-songwriter instinct underneath the gloss.

Langley pulls the other direction. Her records have more twang and grit in them, a Texas-tinged traditionalism that shows up in the way she phrases a hook and the company she keeps — the Miranda Lambert co-sign, the Riley Green duet, the *Dandelion* album's honky-tonk bones. Moroney writes the aftermath; Langley more often writes the night itself. Neither is better, but a fan who loves one does not automatically love the other, and that is the useful thing to know.

## Chart history

For a stretch of 2026 they made history together: two women known primarily for country music topping the singles and albums worlds at essentially the same time. Langley's "Choosin' Texas" turned into one of the longest-running No. 1s the Hot 100 has seen, an out-of-nowhere crossover that kept country at the top of the all-genre chart for months. Moroney's answer came on the album side — *Cloud 9* became her first No. 1 album, topping both the Billboard 200 and the Top Country Albums chart, helped along by multiple retail editions.

So the honest read is that they peaked on different chart battlefields. Langley owned the singles conversation; Moroney planted her flag as an album artist. Calling one the winner misses that they were mostly not competing for the same trophy.

## The 2026 awards split

Until this fall the awards story looked even. Then it forked. Moroney led the 2026 ACM Awards nominations earlier in the year — nine nods — and looked like the season's frontrunner. But when the 2026 CMA nominations landed in September, she was largely left out, a snub that stunned a lot of fans given that *Cloud 9* had topped the charts. Langley, meanwhile, came out on top of the CMA field. We tracked that morning in our post on [Ella leading the 2026 CMA nominations](/news/2026-09-11-ella-langley-leads-2026-cma-nominations), and it is the single biggest reason the two artists' 2026 stories now read so differently despite starting so similarly.

## Who should you see live?

If you want a show that plays like a night out — bar-anthem energy, a band that swings, a set built to be sung back — Langley is the ticket, and she is on the road right now behind *Dandelion*; you can check dates on our [Ella Langley tour page](/tour). If you want the confessional, sing-until-you-cry kind of night, Moroney's your artist. Plenty of fans happily do both, and honestly, that is the correct answer.

## The bottom line

"Ella Langley vs. Megan Moroney" is a false fight dressed up as a rivalry. They arrived close together and they are both proof that country's center of gravity moved toward young women in 2026, but they are aiming at different targets — Langley at the singles chart and the traditional-leaning fan, Moroney at the album and the pop-country diarist. The awards season finally gave the "versus" some teeth, and right now the momentum is Langley's. If you are just discovering one, the smartest move is to give the other an honest listen.
