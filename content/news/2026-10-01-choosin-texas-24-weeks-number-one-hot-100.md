Every Monday morning for most of this year we have checked the same number, and most Mondays it has not budged. On the Billboard Hot 100 dated October 3, "Choosin' Texas" is still sitting exactly where it has camped all year: No. 1. That makes **24 weeks at the top of the Hot 100** for Ella Langley's heartbreak smash — and since she already passed the all-time record a couple of weeks back, every new week is now the longest No. 1 run in the chart's 67-year history, extended by one.

## How Many Weeks Has "Choosin' Texas" Been No. 1?

The short answer, as of the October 3 chart: **24 weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100.** That is more than any song has ever spent on top of the all-genre chart. Langley took sole possession of the record at [23 weeks on the September 22 chart](/news/2026-09-22-choosin-texas-23-weeks-breaks-hot-100-record), passing the mark she had [tied with Mariah Carey a week earlier](/news/2026-09-16-choosin-texas-ties-mariah-carey-hot-100-record). The 24th week is not a new record category — it is the same record, pushed one notch further out of reach.

## The Songs It Had to Pass

To understand why 24 weeks is absurd, look at the company "Choosin' Texas" left behind. As the Associated Press laid out when the record fell, Langley's single climbed over the longest runs by Shaboozey's "A Bar Song (Tipsy)," Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road," and Whitney Houston's "I Will Always Love You" on its way up. The one it finally beat was the biggest of all: Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You," which had held the all-time record at 22 weeks. A midtempo country breakup song released in October 2025 now outranks a Christmas institution that comes back every single December.

## Why a "Chill" Country Song Keeps Winning

What makes the run strange is that "Choosin' Texas" has almost none of the features that usually build a 24-week No. 1. It is specific where hits are usually universal, and mellow where they are usually big. USC musicologist Nate Sloan, speaking to the AP, described Langley's phrasing as "almost like a trance" and filed the track under the "sad banger" tradition — something heartbroken dressed up as something you can drive to. Rhodes College professor Charles L. Hughes, author of "Country Soul," pointed out that the song "has no big moments," and that this is exactly the point: it sounds country without tripping the alarms of listeners who think they dislike country.

The other half of the answer is where it lives. Spotify senior editor Claire Heinichen told the AP the song started on the platform's "Hot Country" list and crossed into "Today's Top Hits," its biggest global flagship playlist, because it slots just as easily into "chill" and "cookout" moods as into country. Luminate counted 911 million U.S. on-demand streams and 1.26 billion globally by mid-August — the kind of passive, everywhere-at-once consumption that keeps a modern song at No. 1 long after a traditional radio hit would have faded. You can read our own [breakdown of the lyrics and the story behind the title](/news/2026-09-10-choosin-texas-meaning-story-behind-song) for why the writing holds up to that many repeat plays.

## It's Not Even Langley's Only Milestone This Fall

Here is the part that still gets us: "Choosin' Texas" is not the only crown she is wearing. Just last week we wrote that ["Be Her" is the best-selling song released in 2026](/news/2026-09-30-ella-langley-be-her-best-selling-song-2026) — a different Langley single topping a different measure of success. She also [leads the 2026 CMA Awards field](/news/2026-09-11-ella-langley-leads-2026-cma-nominations) heading into that ceremony. The AP framed the bigger arc best: Langley went from under a million monthly Spotify listeners around her 2023 Opry debut to [north of 32 million now](/news/2026-06-04-ella-langley-30-million-spotify-listeners), one of the fastest climbs country has seen.

And she is not a one-song story. "Choosin' Texas" has Miranda Lambert among its four co-writers, her [Riley Green duet "You Look Like You Love Me"](/songs/you-look-like-you-love-me) already won awards back in 2024, and the ["Dandelion" album](/songs/dandelion) it all sits on has fed the charts for months.
