If there's one thread running through Ella Langley's rise, it's the company she keeps on a track. For all the solo singles, some of her biggest moments — her first CMA trophy, her first Hot 100 No. 1, her first country-rap crossover — arrived as duets and collaborations. So we pulled every major Ella Langley collaboration into one place: what each song is, who she sang it with, and how far it climbed. If you're building a playlist or just trying to catch up, this is the whole map of her team-ups without the hunting.

## "You Look Like You Love Me" with Riley Green

This is the one that cracked the door open. The call-and-response duet with Riley Green turned a bar-close flirtation into Langley's first real crossover hit and became the song people still name first when they place her. The awards backed it up: it won Musical Event of the Year at the 2024 CMA Awards, then came back in 2025 to sweep Single of the Year, Song of the Year and Music Video of the Year, and it took Single of the Year and Musical Event of the Year at the ACM Awards too, [per Wikipedia's awards history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Langley). If you only know one Ella Langley duet, it's probably this one — start at ["You Look Like You Love Me"](/songs/you-look-like-you-love-me).

## "I Can't Love You Anymore" with Morgan Wallen

The Morgan Wallen pairing is the one that scaled. "I Can't Love You Anymore" reached No. 1 on Billboard's Country Airplay chart, [as Whiskey Riff reported](https://www.whiskeyriff.com/2026/08/07/ella-langley-morgan-wallen-score-1-hit-on-country-airplay-chart-with-i-cant-love-you-anymore/), and it's one of the biggest hits of her career to date. It's also live in the awards conversation right now: it's up for Musical Event of the Year at the [2026 CMA Awards on November 18](/news/2026-11-03-ella-langley-cma-awards-2026-how-to-watch-will-she-perform), one of the nine nominations that put her at the top of the field. Hear it at ["I Can't Love You Anymore"](/songs/i-cant-love-you-anymore).

## "Choosin' Texas" — co-written with Miranda Lambert

Not every collaboration is a duet. "Choosin' Texas" is a Langley solo single, but she wrote and produced it alongside Miranda Lambert, [according to Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Langley) — and the two of them have traded it back and forth on stage all year. It became her first Hot 100 No. 1 and then kept going: it [returned to the top for a record 26th week](/news/2026-11-03-choosin-texas-26-weeks-number-one-hot-100), the longest non-holiday run the chart has ever seen. The Miranda Lambert fingerprints are all over the writing, down to the real-life story that inspired it. Dig into ["Choosin' Texas"](/songs/choosin-texas).

## "Hell at Night" with BigXthaPlug

This is the swing nobody saw coming. "Hell at Night" dropped August 8, 2025 as a country-rap record with BigXthaPlug, and it put Langley's voice on a hip-hop single without softening either side of it. It peaked at No. 26 on the Hot 100 and No. 8 on Hot Country Songs, and the RIAA has since certified it Platinum, [per Wikipedia's chart history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_at_Night). We broke down the petty, revenge-minded story at the center of it in our [full "Hell at Night" explainer](/news/2026-11-01-ella-langley-hell-at-night-meaning); the short version is that both singers refuse to forgive their exes and mean it. Stream ["Hell at Night"](/songs/hell-at-night).

## "Jaded" with Koe Wetzel

Langley and Koe Wetzel first linked up on 2023's "That's Why We Fight," so when they reunited for "Jaded" in 2026 it landed as a rematch between two voices built for friction, [as Country Now described the reunion](https://countrynow.com/koe-wetzel-and-ella-langley-have-fans-buzzing-after-reuniting-for-new-duet-jaded/). It's a sparring match on record — her clean twang against his ragged edge — and it's the collaboration that proves she can hold her own with a harder-rocking partner. We dug into what it's about in our ["Jaded" meaning breakdown](/news/2026-09-15-jaded-ella-langley-koe-wetzel-meaning), or go straight to ["Jaded"](/songs/jaded).

## "Here for the Party" with Gretchen Wilson

The most fun of the bunch is a passing of the torch. Gretchen Wilson tapped Langley for a re-recorded duet of her 2004 anthem "Here for the Party," which came out June 26, 2026 as part of a duets edition of Wilson's debut album. The two of them opened the 2026 CMA Fest broadcast with it, [per Country Now](https://countrynow.com/gretchen-wilson-ella-langley-kick-off-2026-cma-fest-broadcast-with-rowdy-here-for-the-party-duet/) — a rowdy, generation-bridging moment that put Langley next to one of the women who built the lane she's running in now. Hear her take on ["Here for the Party"](/songs/here-for-the-party).

## What all these collaborations say about her

Line them up and a pattern shows: Langley picks partners who pull her somewhere new instead of safe. A traditionalist on the Riley Green duet, the genre's biggest star on the Morgan Wallen single, a rapper on "Hell at Night," a Texas-country firebrand on "Jaded," and a 2000s icon on "Here for the Party." She sounds like herself on all of them, which is the whole trick.
