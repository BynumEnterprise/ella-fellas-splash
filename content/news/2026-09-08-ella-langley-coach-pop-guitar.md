Every so often a story comes out of an Ella Langley show that has nothing to do with the charts, and this is one of them. When Ella Langley came home to Alabama for her sold-out shows at Auburn, she pulled her old teacher backstage and handed him a guitar — the same thing he once did for her, years before anyone outside Hope Hull knew her name. If you have followed her at all, you know Coach Pop is the reason there was a guitar in the story to begin with.

His real name is Maury Popwell, and he has been teaching for 57 years, 22 of them at Hooper Academy in Hope Hull, the small Alabama community Ella grew up in. Per [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/years-after-her-teacher-gave-her-his-guitar-to-chase-her-nashville-dream-ella-langley-returns-the-favor/), the two of them had a standing Friday-morning ritual: Ella would get to school early with her guitar, and she and Coach Pop would play together before the day started. That is the kind of detail that explains a lot about how a kid from a town most people can't find on a map ends up selling out arenas.

## The guitar that started it — and the one that paid it back

The part that gets you is what he did when she left. When Ella packed up for Nashville, Coach Pop gave her the 12-string they had played on all those Friday mornings — not a loaner, but the guitar itself, sent along to chase a dream that had no guarantees attached to it. She has carried that origin with her ever since; it is the quiet version of the same leaving-home story she turned into a hit with ["choosin' texas"](/songs/choosin-texas).

So when she rolled back through Alabama on the [Dandelion Tour](/tour) for back-to-back sold-out nights at Auburn's Neville Arena, she closed the loop. As [Whiskey Riff](https://www.whiskeyriff.com/2026/09/01/ella-langley-presents-her-mentor-coach-pop-with-custom-guitar-years-after-he-gave-her-his-guitar-when-she-moved-to-nashville/) reported, she surprised Coach Pop backstage with a guitar of his own — not the 12-string he gave away, but a custom acoustic made for him, signed by Ella and carrying the artwork from her album *Dandelion*. A teacher gives a student his guitar; two decades later the student, now one of the biggest names in country music, hands one back. It is hard to script better than that.

## Why this one lands harder than a chart post

We write a lot here about tickets and set times and record weeks, and all of that is real. But the throughline of Ella's whole career is that she never stopped sounding like where she came from. Her breakout, ["weren't for the wind"](/songs/werent-for-the-wind), is a small-town song built on small-town wisdom, and the reason it rang true is that it wasn't a costume — it was Hope Hull, the Friday mornings, the 57-years-in-a-classroom kind of people who shaped her. The Coach Pop moment isn't a side note to that story. It basically is that story.

It also fits a pattern anyone who has been to a Dandelion Tour stop recognizes. The shows are big and loud and full of pyro, but the moments people talk about afterward are almost always the small, human ones — a dedication, a hometown shout, a face in the front row she recognizes. If you are heading to one of the remaining nights, that is the thing to watch for.
