If you were one of the people typing "ella langley setlist newark" into your phone this morning, we've got you. Ella brought the Dandelion Tour into the Prudential Center on Thursday night, September 10, and the room was sold out and about as loud as an arena gets for a headliner who was opening festival stages a couple of years ago. We were watching the clips roll in all night, and here's what the show looked like.

## What happened

This was a full headline night, not a support slot, and you could feel the difference in how she paced it. Doors were at 6, Ella was on around 9 after openers **Gabriella Rose** and **Muscadine Bloodline** warmed the room up — the Alabama duo has been filling the direct-support slot on this leg while ERNEST takes some time off — and she used the whole stage the way you do when the night is yours. Newark is a tough, fun crowd, and by the time the duets hit, the upper bowl was singing loud enough that you could hear it over the PA in the fan videos.

## The setlist

Setlists on this leg have been remarkably consistent, so if you missed the top of the show, here's the shape of the Dandelion Tour set the way it's been running night to night. She leans on the new record and then pays off the *Hungover*-era hits that got everybody in the door:

- loving life again
- weren't for the wind
- nicotine
- be her
- dandelion
- paint the town blue
- don't mind if i do
- you look like you love me
- never met anyone like you
- It Wasn't God Who Made Honky Tonk Angels (the Kitty Wells classic)
- Choosin' Texas

If you were there and the order was different, or she slipped in something off the record we didn't catch, tell us — we update these when fans send in the receipts.

## The moments

The stretch everybody replays is the run into "you look like you love me." It's been the loudest crowd moment at every stop on this tour, and Newark was no exception — a full arena taking the second verse off her hands. "Choosin' Texas" to close was the other one; watching a room this size sing a song that spent all year at No. 1 back at her is the kind of thing that still doesn't feel normal for an artist this early in the headline chapter.

And credit to the openers. Gabriella Rose has been on this run long enough that she's got her own pockets of fans singing along now, and Muscadine Bloodline — the Alabama duo covering the direct-support slot while ERNEST is off — is exactly the kind of easy, rowdy set that gets a country crowd ready without stepping on the headliner.
