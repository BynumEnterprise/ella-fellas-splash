**"Choosin' Texas" has now spent 28 weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100** — a record run that no longer has anything close to it in the rearview mirror. On the chart dated November 21, which Billboard posted on November 17, Ella Langley's smash held the top spot for a 28th week and extended the longest No. 1 run in the chart's history. [Billboard's Gary Trust reported](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/articles/ella-langley-choosin-texas-holds-185818775.html) the milestone, and it arrived alongside a brand-new record on a different chart entirely.

## The new record: longest No. 1 ever on Streaming Songs

When we [wrote about the 26-week mark](/news/2026-11-03-choosin-texas-26-weeks-number-one-hot-100) two weeks ago, "Choosin' Texas" had just tied Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You" for the most weeks ever at No. 1 on Billboard's Streaming Songs chart. It has now passed it. With a 27th week on top of Streaming Songs, the song holds that all-time record outright — the first to clear Carey's 26-week mark, with Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road" (20 weeks) the next name on the list.

## The Hot 100 record keeps stretching

Twenty-eight weeks at No. 1 on the Hot 100 is the all-time mark, and the gap behind it is enormous. The next-longest runs belong to "All I Want for Christmas Is You" (22 weeks), then Shaboozey's "A Bar Song (Tipsy)" and "Old Town Road" (19 each). "Choosin' Texas" first reached No. 1 back in February 2026 and has led in nine different calendar months. For the full accounting, see our [Ella Langley Billboard records](/guides/ella-langley-billboard-records) page, which we started when the song [broke the Hot 100 record at 23 weeks](/news/2026-09-22-choosin-texas-23-weeks-breaks-hot-100-record) in September.

## One week from yet another all-time record

There is one more record within reach. "Choosin' Texas" is now in its 49th week atop Hot Country Songs. The all-time mark there is 50 weeks, set by Bebe Rexha and Florida Georgia Line's "Meant to Be." One more week on top — which looks likely — ties it, and a second breaks it outright. The song is also on a record-extending 21st week at No. 1 on Digital Song Sales.

## The numbers are softening — and December is coming

The run is still historic, but the week-to-week figures are easing off their peak. In the tracking week of November 6-12, "Choosin' Texas" drew 17.8 million official U.S. streams (down 5 percent) and 48.7 million in radio airplay audience (down 8 percent), with 4,000 downloads sold (up 7 percent). That is still No. 1 by a comfortable margin, but the direction is what matters heading into December, when holiday songs flood streaming and "All I Want for Christmas Is You" makes its annual climb back to the top of the Hot 100. To keep the streak alive through the holidays, "Choosin' Texas" will have to hold off the very song whose records it just broke.

The near-term timing helps. The 2026 CMA Awards air November 18, where "Choosin' Texas" is nominated for Single, Song and Music Video of the Year, so Langley will be on national TV during a week the song is still No. 1.
