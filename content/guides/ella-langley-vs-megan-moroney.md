If you've spent any time in country music over the last two years, you've had this argument — or heard it at a tailgate. Ella Langley vs. Megan Moroney: two Southern women, both barely into their thirties, both writing their own songs, both stacking No. 1s and New Artist trophies at a pace nobody's matched since the mid-2010s. They get lumped together constantly, and it drives some of us a little crazy, because once you actually sit with both catalogs they're not the same artist at all. Here's the honest, side-by-side breakdown — where they overlap, where they split, and who to reach for depending on the night you're having.

## Ella Langley vs. Megan Moroney at a glance

The short version: Megan Moroney is the polished heartbreak specialist — glittery, wounded, self-deprecating, the friend who cries in the club and looks good doing it. Ella Langley is the barroom brawler — grittier, funnier, more honky-tonk, the one who buys the next round and starts an argument with the ex across the bar. Both are elite writers. They just aim at different corners of the same feeling.

If you want the wider field these two came up in, we mapped the whole generation in our [Ella Langley vs. the class of 2026 field guide](/guides/ella-langley-vs-the-class-of-2026). This piece zooms all the way in on the head-to-head everyone actually wants settled.

## Where they came from

Ella Langley is from Hope Hull, Alabama, a speck of a town just south of Montgomery. Her breakout came the hard way — years of writing, a run of singles, and then "You Look Like You Love Me," her duet with Riley Green off *Hungover*, which turned into the song that put her name on every country playlist in the country.

Megan Moroney is a Georgia girl, raised outside Atlanta and later a University of Georgia student who got discovered by Chase Rice while she was still figuring out whether music was a real plan. Her spark was "Tennessee Orange" in the fall of 2022 — released independently, the story of wearing an enemy team's colors for a boy, and a song that earned her a record deal on the strength of streaming alone.

So both are self-made Southern women who broke through on a single undeniable song. The difference is temperament, and you can hear it from the first bar.

## The sound: honky-tonk grit vs. sparkly heartbreak

This is the real dividing line. Megan Moroney built her lane on what fans (and Megan herself) call "emo cowgirl" country — clean, contemporary production, big radio choruses, and lyrics that live inside one very specific feeling: the ache of wanting someone who isn't good for you. "Tennessee Orange," "I'm Not Pretty," "Am I Okay?" — they're vulnerable, self-aware, and produced to shine.

Ella Langley pulls harder toward tradition. There's more twang in her voice, more honky-tonk in the arrangements, and a willingness to be rowdy and blunt that Megan mostly sidesteps. Ella writes heartbreak too, but she writes it mad, or drunk, or laughing at herself — and she loves a co-ed duet, trading verses with Riley Green and Koe Wetzel where Megan tends to hold the mic alone. If Megan is the 2 a.m. text you regret, Ella is the parking-lot confrontation that follows it.

## The 2026 chart years

Here's where the "they're the same" argument really falls apart — because in 2026 they each had a monster year, but in different weight classes.

Megan Moroney released her third album, *Cloud 9*, which entered the Billboard 200 at No. 1 in February 2026 and featured duets with Ed Sheeran and Kacey Musgraves — a clear play at crossover scale, per Taste of Country. It cemented her as a genuine album-seller with pop reach.

Ella Langley's 2026 was, statistically, one of the biggest breakout years country has seen in a decade. Her sophomore album *Dandelion* debuted at No. 1, and its single "Choosin' Texas" went all the way to No. 1 on the all-genre Billboard Hot 100 and stayed there for weeks — a feat almost no country song by a woman has managed. Then she swept all seven of her nominations at the 2026 ACM Awards, a single-year record, per Variety. Follow the whole run on our [tour hub and record tracker](/tour).

Both won early, too: Megan took ACM New Female Artist in 2023 and CMA New Artist of the Year in 2024, while Ella collected CMA hardware in 2025 before her ACM sweep. Two New Artist winners, back to back — which is exactly why the comparison won't die.

## So who should you listen to?

Reach for **Megan Moroney** when you want the feeling handled with care — polished, sad, a little glamorous, easy to sing along to on a long drive. Start with "Tennessee Orange," then "Am I Okay?"

Reach for **Ella Langley** when you want the feeling handled with a slammed screen door — grittier, funnier, more honky-tonk, and built for a room full of people yelling the words back. Start with "You Look Like You Love Me," then "Choosin' Texas."

The truth most fans land on eventually: they're not rivals, they're complements. Megan is the wound; Ella is the fight that caused it. Put both on the same playlist and you've basically got a full breakup, start to finish.
