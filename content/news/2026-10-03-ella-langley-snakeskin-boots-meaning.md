The wait is over: "Snakeskin Boots," the Ella Langley and Miranda Lambert duet we flagged a couple weeks back, went live October 2 as the opening track of Lambert's new album *Crisco*. If you came here looking for the meaning of "Snakeskin Boots," the short version is this: it is a swaggering, retro-Western story song about a woman who moves through life fast and leaves no loose ends — Thelma and Louise if they never drove off the cliff. The longer version, and why this particular pairing matters so much for Ella, is worth a few minutes.

## What "Snakeskin Boots" Is About

The song follows a woman with somewhere to be and no patience for anyone slowing her down — admired and a little feared, the kind of character country has always loved to build a three-minute Western around. Lambert has been clear about where the idea came from. "What if Thelma and Louise didn't drive off the cliff, and what would they be getting into?" she said of the writing, describing it as two women "getting away with something." The chorus leans into outlaw imagery — a hangman's noose slipped at the last second — without ever tipping into cartoon. It is a warning and a wink at the same time.

What keeps it from being a novelty is the restraint in the performances. Langley and Lambert do not oversing it; they trade lines like two people who already know how the story ends and are enjoying watching you figure it out.

## The Sound: "Western Disco"

Musically this is the most unusual thing either singer has put out this year. Lambert has said she wanted it to feel like Nancy Sinatra's "Bang Bang" — late-'60s, cinematic, a little dangerous — the kind of track that could [score a Tarantino scene as easily as it could fill a dance floor at Studio 54](https://country.iheart.com/content/2026-10-02-miranda-lambert-ella-langley-bring-retro-western-swagger-to-cinematic-snakeskin-boots/). [Whiskey Riff's first-listen review landed on the perfect two-word label for it](https://www.whiskeyriff.com/2026/09/24/first-listen-miranda-lambert-and-ella-langleys-duet-snakeskin-boots-is-western-disco-perfection/): "Western disco." Spaghetti-Western guitar, a pulse you can move to, and two voices built for the drama. It is a long way from the barroom heartbreak of Ella's own catalog, which is the point — this is a costume both singers clearly loved putting on.

## Who Wrote It

Lambert wrote "Snakeskin Boots" with Natalie Hemby, Brendan McLoughlin and Jesse Frasure, and she has said the song was built with Ella in mind from the start — this is Langley stepping onto Lambert's record as the featured guest, a reversal of their last duet. *Crisco* is Lambert's first studio album in nearly two years, and she front-loaded it: Ella opens the record, and Chris Stapleton turns up later on "A Song to Sing." Handing the first track to Langley is its own kind of statement.

## Why This Is a Full-Circle Moment for Ella

To understand why fans care, you have to rewind. Lambert co-produced Langley's *Dandelion* album and has been a working mentor to her across the whole project. The two already share one of the year's best duets in ["Butterfly Season,"](/news/2026-09-20-ella-langley-butterfly-season-meaning) the hopeful closer on *Dandelion*, and Lambert co-wrote and co-produced ["Choosin' Texas,"](/songs/choosin-texas) the single that spent a record-breaking run at No. 1 all year. "Snakeskin Boots" flips the roles: where Lambert was the guest on Ella's record, now Ella is the guest on hers.

Lambert did not hedge about what she thinks this means. Talking about the collaboration, she called Langley ["our next legend."](https://www.audacy.com/national/music/miranda-lambert-ella-langley) Coming from the artist who has set the template for headstrong country women for two decades, that is not a small thing to say out loud — and putting Ella first on the album is Lambert backing it up.
