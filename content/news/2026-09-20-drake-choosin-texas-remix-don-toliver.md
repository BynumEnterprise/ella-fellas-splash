We've watched Drake orbit "Choosin' Texas" all summer — spinning it in a Houston DJ set, singing along on the big screen at the Little Rock show, dropping a "Goated Pen" comment in Ella's TikTok replies. This week it finally turned into an actual song, and the rollout has been strange enough that it's worth laying out exactly what happened.

On Tuesday, September 15, Drake unveiled a project he's calling *Fear of Missing Out (FOMO)* in a livestream — less a traditional album than a run of videos and songs. Tucked inside was the long-rumored "Choosin' Texas" remix, this one featuring Don Toliver. Per HITS Daily Double, Drake slows the original down into a hazy, R&B-leaning rework and swaps most of Ella's lyrics for his own, including a Johnny Manziel line. It is, as you'd guess, nowhere close to country.

Here's the part that keeps it in limbo: the remix has not been officially released to streaming. What's floating around is captured off the one-time livestream. And then over the weekend it got stranger — Whiskey Riff reported on September 19 that Drake re-uploaded the FOMO video with Ella Langley's footage removed. So the version that briefly existed with her in it is gone, and there's still no clean, credited release anyone can add to a playlist.

Why does that matter for the record books? Because "Choosin' Texas" just [tied Mariah Carey's all-time Hot 100 mark at 22 weeks at No. 1](/news/2026-09-16-choosin-texas-ties-mariah-carey-hot-100-record) — a run Ella built with no featured artist and no remix, which is the rarest part of the whole story. As Saving Country Music put it, the fact Drake has *held off* on a proper release is arguably the best-case scenario: nobody can credibly claim the song needed an outside push to get where it is.

We'll say the obvious thing. It's genuinely cool that a song this big pulls in a fan like Drake. But this is Ella's moment, and country music's, and it got here on the strength of the original. Whatever the remix eventually becomes, we're not in a hurry for it.
