Somewhere in the middle of a sold-out Dandelion Tour show this week, Ella Langley stopped between songs to talk about gas station taquitos. It sounds like a bit, and it started as one, but it turned into the kind of between-song speech her fans have started showing up early for. Per [Holler](https://holler.country/news/breaking/ella-langley-reflects-on-how-shes-changed-over-the-years/), Langley used the moment to thank the broke, exhausted, van-riding version of herself who got her to a stage this size — and to tell the room to do the same for their own past selves.

## What she actually said

The joke came first. Now that the tours are bigger and the travel is more comfortable, she told the crowd, life looks different: "I don't drink as much tequila and I'm not eating gas station taquitos every day, you know, I feel like it's a good change in my life." Then she turned it around. "And then I start to see that girl... If it was not for her, eating gas station taquitos in the van at 2am, and busting her ass to get to where she is today, I wouldn't be in front of a sold-out crowd tonight."

From there it stopped being about her. "I don't want you to ever let somebody make you feel guilty for who you were," she told the audience, "because that person took you to where you are right now." She asked everyone in the building to send a little gratitude backward: "You need to thank that person. Thank you so much for getting me here, thank you so much for being tough for me, thank you so much for being strong."

## Why it's more earned than it looks

The line that makes the speech hit is the one about the van at 2am, and it isn't a metaphor. To anyone who found Langley through "Choosin' Texas," she can look like an overnight arrival. She isn't. She's been releasing music since 2017 and spent years on bar gigs and small rooms before opening for artists like Jon Pardi and Morgan Wallen and breaking through on her own terms. The taquitos-in-the-van years were real, and they were long. That's the whole point of thanking the girl who lived them.

These pauses have quietly become part of the show. Earlier this run, [a speech about faith and mental health went viral](/news/2026-07-18-ella-langley-viral-stage-speeches-mental-health), and the prayers she's talked about on stage are the same ones that shaped ["Speaking Terms"](/news/2026-07-31-ella-langley-dove-awards-speaking-terms). It's a through-line: the songs and the speeches are coming from the same place.

It also lands differently against the week she's having. "Choosin' Texas" just became the [longest-running No. 1 in Billboard Hot 100 history](/news/2026-09-22-choosin-texas-23-weeks-breaks-hot-100-record), so the sold-out room she's thanking her younger self in front of is, right now, the biggest stage in the format.
