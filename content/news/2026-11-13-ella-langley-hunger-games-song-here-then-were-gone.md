Ella Langley has a brand-new song out today, and it is not the one anybody expected. Her Hunger Games song, "Here Then We're Gone," landed November 13 on *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping (The Album)*, the 29-track soundtrack to the next film in the franchise. It is a pensive, acoustic country number about grief and moving forward, [as Rolling Stone described it](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/ella-langley-here-then-were-gone-hunger-games-single-1235657421/) — a long way from the honky-tonk swing and the chart-eating singles that defined her 2026. If you want the quiet, writerly side of Langley, this is it.

## What "Here Then We're Gone" is about

The song is a reflection on losing someone and finding a way to keep going — the same emotional lane as the hushed ballads that made Nashville take her seriously in the first place. Rolling Stone calls it "pensive" and "acoustic," and the writing credits back that up: Langley wrote it with Aaron Raitiere and Ernest, two of the most in-demand pens in town, and **Dave Cobb** produced it.

That Cobb connection matters. Cobb executive produced the whole soundtrack, and he is the Grammy-winning producer behind some of the most stripped-back, roots-forward records of the last decade. Handing him an acoustic grief song is the opposite of chasing a hit, and that is the point — this is Langley the songwriter, not Langley the radio juggernaut.

If this is the sound you came for, it has a clear lineage in her catalog. Start with [weren't for the wind](/songs/werent-for-the-wind), the quiet solo single that convinced the industry she was the real thing, then [loving life again](/songs/loving-life-again), the recovery song she wrote after stepping off the road in 2025. "Here Then We're Gone" sits right next to both of them.

## The soundtrack: 29 songs, Dave Cobb, and a wild guest list

*The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping (The Album)* arrived November 13 through MCA Records with 29 tracks, [per Taste of Country](https://tasteofcountry.com/hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-ella-langley-kacey-musgraves/). It is a deliberately cross-genre collection, and the company Langley is keeping on it tells you how far she has come in two years.

The country contingent alone is stacked: alongside Langley's "Here Then We're Gone," there is Kacey Musgraves ("Ruthless"), Tyler Childers ("Old Money"), Stephen Wilson Jr. ("First in Line"), and Carter Faith ("Can't Let You Go"). Outside the genre, the record pulls in Stevie Nicks, Noah Kahan, Noah Cyrus, The Smashing Pumpkins, Depeche Mode, and The Cure, [as Backstage Country laid out](https://backstagecountry.com/2026/11/03/from-ella-langley-to-the-cure-meet-the-artists-behind-the-new-hunger-games-soundtrack/). Even cast members got in on it, with contributions tied to Jennifer Lawrence, McKenna Grace, and Whitney Peak.

Hunger Games soundtracks have a history of launching career-defining moments — Taylor Swift's "Safe & Sound" came off the very first one back in 2012 — so a slot on this record is a genuine stamp of arrival. For Langley, it is also the latest in a run of high-profile features that sit outside her own albums, from the Riley Green and Morgan Wallen duets to her guest spots across 2026. We keep a full rundown in [every Ella Langley duet and collaboration explained](/news/2026-11-04-ella-langley-duets-collaborations).

## When the movie comes out

*The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping* hits theaters **November 20, 2026**, a week after the soundtrack. The film is a prequel that follows a young Haymitch Abernathy (played by Joseph Zada) through the 50th Hunger Games, decades before the Katniss story. Dropping the album a week ahead of the premiere is the usual playbook — it lets songs like "Here Then We're Gone" find an audience before the movie gives them a scene to live in.

## Why this is a bigger deal than one soundtrack cut

It is easy to file a soundtrack song under "nice side project," but the timing says otherwise. Langley heads into the week as one of the most-nominated artists of awards season — her "Choosin' Texas" has rewritten the Hot 100 record books, and she is deep in the [Billboard record-breaking tally](/guides/ella-langley-billboard-records) we keep updating. Dropping a somber, Dave Cobb–produced acoustic ballad in the middle of all that is a flex of range: proof she can score a No. 1 party anthem and then turn around and sell quiet grief on a blockbuster soundtrack.

If you are new to how wide that range actually runs, our ranking of [every Ella Langley song](/guides/best-ella-langley-songs-ranked) is the place to hear it, and [be her](/songs/be-her) — the steel-soaked ballad that peaked at No. 2 behind only her own "Choosin' Texas" — is the closest cousin to what she does on "Here Then We're Gone." Play them back to back and you will hear why a Hunger Games soundtrack came calling.
